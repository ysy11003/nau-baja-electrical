Overview

Why the electrical system matters

Baja SAE is an international competition run by SAE International in which engineering students design and build a single-seat off-road vehicle that must endure harsh terrain while meeting the Baja SAE rules. The electrical system supports the brake light, the engine kill switches and the digital display, so it has to be durable, serviceable and properly integrated into the vehicle.

Our objective is a safe, reliable and serviceable system: a main wiring harness, power distribution with circuit protection, engine kill switches, a brake light, and the connections between them. The design keeps weight down, reduces failure points, and leaves room for a digital dashboard, more sensors and data logging.

Sizing starts from the basics: Ohm's law and the power equation give each load's current, which sets wire gauge and fuse ratings while accounting for voltage drop, wire resistance and grounding.

Subsystems

What we have done so far

P01-1 · P01-2

Main wiring harness & power distribution

A 12 V sealed lead-acid AGM battery feeds a 25 A primary fuse and a fuse distribution block, which splits power into three separately fused circuits: the digital display, the hydraulic brake pressure switches, and the relay / brake light.

When the footbrake builds pressure, a pressure switch closes and energizes a normally open automotive relay, which switches power to the brake light.

  • Researched hydraulic brake pressure switches
  • Selected relay and brake light
  • Wiring diagram and wire sizing
  • Battery type chosen per Baja SAE rules
  • Select the specific battery model

Isolated circuit

Engine kill switch system

Two Polaris kill switches are wired to the ignition kill lead of the Kohler CH440 engine. One sits in the cockpit within the driver's reach; the other is on the rear exterior of the chassis for track personnel.

Either switch can shut the engine down on its own, and the circuit runs separately from the main harness so the brake light keeps working.

  • Reviewed kill switch rules and safety regulations
  • Selected Polaris switches for the CH440
  • Engine-only wiring schematic
  • Confirm switch specs and mounting
  • Install and test each switch independently

P01-3

Digital dashboard & sensors

The dashboard shows two numbers: vehicle speed and engine RPM. Hall-effect sensors detect wheel and engine rotation, an ESP32-S3 turns pulses into speed and RPM, a Raspberry Pi 5 handles the display information, and a Nextion LCD shows the result.

Two buck converters step the 12 V battery down for the display electronics.

  • Preliminary wiring schematic
  • Draft dashboard interface

Next: system integration

Finalize schematics and confirm fuses, wire sizes and the rest of the parts list, then order components and bench-test each subsystem. The brake light will be tested with the kill switches active, the Hall sensors checked against known speeds, and communication confirmed between the ESP32, Raspberry Pi, sensors and LCD.
